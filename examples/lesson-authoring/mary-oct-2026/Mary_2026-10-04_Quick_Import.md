Lesson Date: 2026-10-04
Lesson Title: Mary | Experiences & Speaking Confidence
Short Introduction: Talk about long friendships, places you want to visit, and feeling more confident when speaking.
Today's Focus: Use I've known, keep statement word order inside questions, and say will be held.
Topics: present perfect, question order, passive voice, speaking confidence

## Common Mistake
Incorrect: I know her for a long time.
Correct: I've known her for a long time.
Japanese: 彼女とは長い付き合いです。
Explanation: Use have known for knowing someone from the past up to now. Use for with a length of time and since with a starting point.
Examples:
I've known her for ten years.
I've known her since high school.

## Useful Phrase
English: I've always wanted to visit that place, but I haven't been there yet.
Japanese: ずっとそこに行ってみたかったけど、まだ行ったことがありません。
Explanation: Haven't been there yet means you have not visited it up to now.

## Grammar
English: Do you know what he is doing?
Japanese: 彼が何をしているか知っていますか？
Explanation: Direct question: What is he doing? Inside a larger sentence, use what he is doing, not what is he doing.
Example: I don't know what he is doing.

## Grammar
English: The seminar will be held tomorrow.
Japanese: そのセミナーは明日開催されます。
Explanation: Hold - held - held. They hold the event; the event is held. For the future, use will be held.
Example: The event is held every year.

## Useful Phrase
English: What helped you feel more confident?
Japanese: 何がきっかけで自信が出てきましたか？
Explanation: Nervous and confident are adjectives. Nervousness and confidence are nouns.
Example: Once I started speaking, I focused on my message and felt less nervous.

## Natural English Upgrade
Original: My instinct is 100%.
Natural: My gut tells me he's coming.
Japanese: 私の勘では、彼は来ると思います。
Explanation: This expresses a gut feeling. For certainty, say: I'm 100% sure he's coming.

## Pronunciation
English: Practice makes perfect.
IPA: practice /ˈpræk.tɪs/ | practise /ˈpræk.tɪs/
Japanese: 「練習すれば上達する」ということわざ。ここでは名詞なので、米英どちらも practice です。
Explanation: US: practice is both a noun and a verb. UK: practice is the noun; practise is the verb. The two spellings sound the same.
Examples:
US: I practice English every day.
UK: I practise English every day.

## Quick Practice

### Multiple Choice
Question: Choose the correct sentence about tomorrow's seminar.
A: The seminar will held tomorrow.
B: The seminar will hold tomorrow.
C: The seminar will be held tomorrow.
Answer: C
Explanation: The passive pattern is will be + past participle: will be held.

### Fill in the Blank
Question: Choose for or since: I've known her ___ high school.
Answer: since
Explanation: High school is the starting point. A length of time would take for.

### Error Correction
Question: Fix the word order: Do you know what is he doing?
Answer: Do you know what he is doing?
Explanation: Inside the larger question, use subject + verb: he is.

### Sentence Reorder
Question: Put the words in order. Start with I.
Items:
- there
- been
- I
- yet.
- haven't
Answer: I haven't been there yet.
Explanation: Have not + been. Yet usually comes at the end here.

### Japanese to English Practice
Question: 「彼女とは長い付き合いです」を英語で。Use known and for a long time.
Answer: I've known her for a long time.
Accepted answers:
I have known her for a long time.
Explanation: The relationship started in the past and continues now.

### Short Answer
Question: What helped you feel less nervous after you started speaking? Give one possible answer; it does not have to be a real experience.
Hint: Once I started speaking, I...
Answer: Once I started speaking, I focused on my message and felt less nervous.
Explanation: Your answer can be different. Explain what changed after you started speaking.

### Self Check
Question: Can you explain why Practice makes perfect uses practice in both American and British English?
Answer: Practice is a noun in this sentence. Both varieties use practice for the noun.
Explanation: British English uses practise only for the verb.
