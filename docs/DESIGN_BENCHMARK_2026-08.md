# Design Benchmark — Adult English Learning Platforms

**Research date:** 2026-08-14

**Purpose:** Inform an original visual and UX direction for Tahmid English Club / English Review Hub v9.

**Scope:** Public, signed-out marketing and pricing experiences on Bizmates, NativeCamp, DMM Eikaiwa, RareJob, and Cambly/Cambly Pro.
**Constraint:** These observations are references, not instructions to copy source code, brand assets, layouts, wording, or claims.

## Method

The official public pages listed below were inspected in the Codex in-app browser. Where the browser permitted it, the review included:

- desktop hierarchy and above-the-fold composition;
- a 390 × 844 mobile viewport;
- computed typography, spacing, card, button, and overflow dimensions;
- accessible DOM structure and visible page copy;
- pricing hierarchy, trust signals, navigation, and visible carousel/media controls.

Prices, promotions, counters, and claims are recorded only as evidence of how the official pages presented information on the research date. They are not independently verified business facts and should not be reused as Tahmid English Club claims.

## Executive synthesis

The strongest original direction for Tahmid English Club is a combination of five ideas:

1. **Bizmates:** lead with the professional outcome and place honest proof immediately after the hero.
2. **NativeCamp:** make one memorable product promise more prominent than the complete feature inventory.
3. **DMM Eikaiwa:** give materially different tiers clear visual identities.
4. **RareJob:** prioritize calm pricing clarity and true single-column mobile behavior.
5. **Cambly:** tell a human journey from goal to support to visible progress.

This combination should still look unmistakably like Tahmid English Club: personal, bilingual, teacher-led, warm, and focused on reviewing real lessons.

## 1. Bizmates

### Official pages inspected

- [Bizmates homepage](https://www.bizmates.jp/)
- [Bizmates pricing](https://www.bizmates.jp/price/)

### Observed desktop design

The official homepage used a corporate blue-and-white system with yellow reserved for conversion actions. Its typography was based on `Noto Sans JP`; inspected navigation and utility text were generally 14px, while major section headings reached approximately 28–36px. The hero used larger emphasized words inside an otherwise compact Japanese headline.

The inspected desktop header was approximately 111px high: a 56px utility row and a 55px primary navigation row. The hero combined:

- a career-oriented result;
- a trainer/founder photograph under a blue overlay;
- a limited introductory offer;
- a prominent free-trial CTA.

Immediately below the hero, the official page displayed satisfaction/member claims, award-like proof graphics, and enterprise logos. This placed reassurance before the long product explanation.

The official pricing page displayed three approximately 339px-wide cards with about 32px between them and 8px corner radii. Each card combined learner fit, introductory price, continuing price, lesson frequency, included services, and a card-specific CTA. The CTA interior used a solid Bizmates blue with bold white text.

### Observed mobile behavior

On the inspected mobile homepage, the navigation collapsed to the logo, corporate/login links, and a menu control. The hero used approximately 20px setup text with larger emphasized words near 44px. The measured homepage document width matched the 390px viewport, so no horizontal overflow was detected in that state.

The accessible structure showed logo and learner-story slide groups. Carousel presence was verified, but autoplay was not independently confirmed.

### Why it works

- The first message sells a business outcome rather than an abstract library of lessons.
- Yellow is scarce and therefore keeps high conversion priority.
- Proof appears early enough to reduce risk before the visitor reads detailed explanations.
- “Recommended for this kind of learner” copy reduces the effort required to choose a plan.
- Introductory and continuing prices are both shown inside the same plan context.

### Original adaptation for Tahmid English Club

- Lead with a specific result such as: **“Remember your real lessons. Speak with confidence.”**
- Follow the hero with an honest proof strip: real lesson count, real activity count, availability of sample lessons, teacher-led feedback, and the privacy promise.
- Give every paid tier one plain-language learner profile before its feature list.
- Use genuine product screens, teacher feedback samples, or purpose-made illustrations instead of adopting a corporate-executive hero.

### Do not copy

- The dense two-row desktop header.
- The exact blue/yellow palette or photographic composition.
- Award graphics, client logos, or percentage claims without genuine evidence and permission.
- A promotion so visually dominant that the normal ongoing price becomes secondary.

## 2. NativeCamp

### Official pages inspected

- [NativeCamp homepage](https://nativecamp.net/)
- [NativeCamp Premium plan](https://nativecamp.net/usage/plan_premium)

### Observed desktop design

The official homepage used a deep navy foundation with orange conversion accents and `Noto Sans JP`. Its inspected campaign headline was approximately 74px on desktop. Standard section headings were approximately 40px.

The page repeated one dominant proposition—high-frequency/unlimited lessons—through the hero, reasons-to-believe sections, a learning-theory explanation, plan presentation, and comparison content. The official pricing presentation separated:

- the main Premium plan;
- the Family plan;
- optional native-speaker and Callan-method additions.

This avoided putting every possible choice into one large card grid.

The official homepage displayed a large user counter, broad tutor-country coverage, explanatory learning content, app features, media references, and learner reviews. The inspected learner-review area included current-looking timestamps on 2026-08-14. These are observations of the page presentation, not independently verified claims.

Carousel controls and repeated review-slide groups were visible in the page structure. Autoplay was not independently verified.

### Observed mobile behavior

At 390px, the campaign heading reduced to approximately 47px and standard section headings to approximately 24px. The principal campaign CTA measured about 357 × 67px. The measured document width was exactly 390px, with no horizontal overflow in the inspected homepage state.

The mobile hierarchy remained promotion-led, followed by the free-trial section and core reasons to choose the product.

### Why it works

- One proposition is repeated consistently enough to become memorable.
- The page explains why frequent practice matters, not only what the subscription includes.
- Base plans and optional additions are visually separated.
- Time-stamped reviews make the service feel active.
- The mobile CTA occupies nearly the full readable width and is easy to tap.

### Original adaptation for Tahmid English Club

- Establish one dominant promise: **“Every real lesson becomes practice you can repeat.”**
- Explain the learning loop visually: real lesson → review → varied practice → teacher feedback → progress.
- Keep Premium+ coaching or submission allowances subordinate to the four main plan choices.
- Use a genuine “recently added” or “this week’s lesson” area to create freshness without fabricating community activity.

### Do not copy

- A temporary campaign as the main long-term brand message.
- The page’s very long, dense sales structure.
- Competitor comparison tables unless every value is dated, sourced, and actively maintained.
- Multiple optional-product names before the learner understands the core Review Hub.

## 3. DMM Eikaiwa

### Official pages inspected

- [DMM Eikaiwa homepage](https://eikaiwa.dmm.com/)
- [DMM Eikaiwa pricing](https://eikaiwa.dmm.com/plan/)

### Observed desktop design

The official pricing experience used a white/cream base. Orange identified the Standard family and blue identified the Plus Native family. Its font stack combined `Open Sans`, `Noto Sans JP`, and Japanese system fonts.

Measured pricing hierarchy included:

- an approximately 35px page heading;
- approximately 28px plan-family headings;
- a 980px-wide plan-family panel in a 1,280px viewport;
- approximately 462px inner option cards in two columns;
- 20px outer-panel and 10px inner-card corner radii.

Plan CTAs measured approximately 195 × 47px. They were pill-shaped and used a darker 4px bottom shadow, giving a tactile, button-like appearance. Each frequency option had its own action instead of relying on one CTA for the entire pricing section.

The official homepage used concrete instructor and catalog proof: material counts, tutor-country coverage, instructor acceptance messaging, ratings, favorite counts, and learner comments. Large campaign banners and repeated tutor/banner slide structures were present.

### Observed mobile behavior

The public pricing page produced the clearest negative benchmark in this review. At a 390px viewport:

- the measured document `scrollWidth` was 1,024px;
- the plan-family panel remained approximately 980px wide;
- a captured screenshot visibly cropped the pricing content and navigation.

The page therefore behaved like a wide desktop layout inside a narrow viewport during the inspection.

### Why it works

- Color separates two materially different product families at a glance.
- Each option presents frequency, total price, campaign price, and duration together.
- Instructor cards make a large tutor marketplace feel human and specific.
- Slight CTA depth improves affordance without requiring elaborate animation.

### Original adaptation for Tahmid English Club

- Use restrained tier accents so Free, Standard, Premium, and Premium+ remain recognizable while sharing one card system.
- Use a subtle 1–2px pressed/hover effect for primary actions rather than reproducing DMM’s exact button shadow.
- Describe each tier through one use case and a compact “adds to the previous tier” list.
- On mobile, stack every plan in one column; do not require a horizontal card carousel.

### Do not copy

- DMM’s ecosystem advertising header or crowded navigation.
- Repeated promotional banners and repeated conversion pressure.
- The exact orange/blue color assignment.
- The fixed-width mobile pricing behavior.

## 4. RareJob

### Official pages inspected

- [RareJob homepage](https://www.rarejob.com/)
- [RareJob conversation course and pricing](https://www.rarejob.com/experiences/course/conversation/)

### Observed desktop design

The inspected official pages used a restrained white/near-black design, a green registration action, and an orange free-trial CTA. The font stack used Helvetica/Arial with Hiragino and Meiryo fallbacks.

The measured hierarchy was deliberately calm:

- 32px regular page heading;
- 24px regular section headings;
- 20px bold subheadings;
- 16px body text with approximately 1.6 line height.

Content sat in an approximately 1,024px desktop column. The pricing container was white with an 8px radius. Four options appeared as approximately 476 × 104px thin-border cards in two columns. Each card paired a small frequency pill with the total monthly price and per-lesson cost.

The official homepage presented cumulative membership, lesson-volume, and corporate-adoption figures, plus learner scenarios, beginner reassurance, and named learning frameworks. These are attributed to the official page and were not independently verified.

Compared with the other inspected sites, the page relied more on static explanation and less on visible carousel mechanics.

### Observed mobile behavior

RareJob produced the clearest positive mobile pricing benchmark:

- pricing container: approximately 358px wide;
- individual cards: approximately 326 × 148px;
- cards stacked vertically;
- primary trial CTA: approximately 324 × 64px;
- measured document `scrollWidth`: exactly 390px.

The information hierarchy survived the transition to mobile without shrinking a desktop comparison table.

### Why it works

- Moderate type sizing and whitespace make the page feel dependable instead of aggressive.
- Thin-border cards keep dense prices calm and scannable.
- Per-lesson cost provides useful context while the real monthly price remains primary.
- Mobile preserves hierarchy and touch-target size.

### Original adaptation for Tahmid English Club

- Use RareJob-like clarity as the baseline for the four-tier plan experience.
- Show the full monthly or six-month total first; show an equivalent monthly value only as supporting context.
- Stack four independent cards on phones and maintain at least 44px tap targets.
- Include a quiet facts panel explaining privacy, teacher-feedback limits, access duration, and exactly what is included.

### Do not copy

- The green/orange color system.
- Pricing placed too late on an extremely long page.
- Generic flat cards that remove Tahmid’s warmer visual personality.
- Institutional metrics that Tahmid English Club cannot accurately support.

## 5. Cambly and Cambly Pro

### Official pages inspected

- [Cambly homepage](https://www.cambly.com/english)
- [Cambly pricing in JPY](https://www.cambly.com/en/subscribe?currency=JPY)
- [Cambly Pro](https://www.cambly.com/english/pro)

### Observed desktop design

The official Cambly homepage used an editorial black/cream/yellow identity rather than a conventional SaaS-blue system. Display headings used `GT Super`.

Measured desktop type included:

- 76px hero headings with approximately 87px line height;
- 44px standard section headings;
- larger statement headings later in the page.

The page told a sequential story: learner goals → finding the right tutors → seeing progress → choosing a plan → learner story. Pill CTAs were generally 64px high. Yellow identified forward learning actions, while black identified plan-specific conversion.

The official plan presentation used progressive inclusion across Small Groups, Private+, and Pro. Higher tiers described what they added rather than repeating every shared capability in the same visual block. The JPY pricing page also exposed plan type, weekly frequency, and subscription-length choices.

Trust was qualitative: tutor expertise, lesson structure, personalized feedback, learner journeys, recordings/transcripts, practice tools, and progress tracking. Rich-media/video panels and learner-avatar controls supplied interaction.

### Observed mobile behavior

At 390px:

- hero and ordinary section headings reduced to about 38px;
- later statement headings used approximately 46–55px;
- the three plan cards stacked vertically at approximately 358px wide;
- cards used 32px padding and 8px radii;
- cards measured approximately 674–752px high because each contained substantial explanatory content;
- the measured document `scrollWidth` was exactly 390px.

Primary mobile actions remained 64px high. Final paired actions expanded to the full 358px content width.

### Why it works

- Editorial typography makes the product feel human and aspirational.
- The goal-to-progress sequence explains the learning experience before asking for a plan decision.
- Progressive inclusion reduces duplicated plan copy.
- Mobile cards remain readable and touch-friendly.

### Original adaptation for Tahmid English Club

- Use a restrained editorial accent only for short English hero statements, paired with a highly readable Japanese sans serif.
- Tell one authentic learner journey from real class to review practice and teacher response.
- Use “Everything in Standard, plus…” and “Everything in Premium, plus…” in the cards, while keeping a complete comparison table below for accessibility and certainty.
- Show a sanitized, authentic teacher-feedback example instead of a generic lifestyle testimonial.

### Do not copy

- Cambly’s exact serif or black/cream/yellow identity.
- 55px mobile headings for long bilingual Japanese copy.
- Large discount anchors or countdown messaging.
- Very tall pricing cards without a compact comparison alternative.

## Recommended Tahmid design direction

### Typography

- UI and Japanese: `Inter`, `Noto Sans JP`, system sans-serif.
- Optional English display accent: one licensed serif used only for short hero/editorial statements.
- Hero: 52–64px desktop; 38–42px mobile.
- H2: 32–36px desktop; 26–30px mobile.
- Body: 16px with 1.65–1.75 line height.
- Labels and metadata: 13–14px, never relied upon for essential information alone.

### Layout and spacing

- Main content width: 1,080–1,120px.
- Section padding: 80–96px desktop; 52–64px mobile.
- Card gap: 20–24px.
- Card radius: 16–20px to preserve Tahmid’s warmer character.
- CTA height: 52–56px; at least 44px for every interactive target.
- Use four plan columns only when each card can remain about 250px or wider; otherwise switch to 2 × 2 and then one column.

### Color

Continue Tahmid’s existing navy/teal identity, warm cream surfaces, and one amber/gold conversion accent. Tier identity should come from restrained accents, labels, and borders—not four unrelated palettes.

### Navigation

- One compact sticky row.
- Keep Plans visible as a primary destination.
- Signed-out desktop priority: brand, Lessons, How it works, Plans, Sign in, Start free.
- Mobile priority: brand, one primary action if space allows, and a menu.
- Logged-in lesson pages should be simpler than the marketing site and protect learning focus.

### Pricing

- Present Free, Standard, Premium, and Premium+ in a stable order.
- Make the monthly/six-month selector unambiguous and preserve the exact billing total.
- Give one tier a “recommended” treatment only if the business genuinely recommends it.
- Keep every card structurally comparable: audience, price, key outcome, additions, limits, CTA.
- Add a full accessible comparison table below the cards.
- Never use a horizontally overflowing desktop price table on mobile.

### Trust

Prefer evidence Tahmid English Club can genuinely support:

- real number of lessons and activities;
- real sample lessons;
- named teacher involvement;
- a clear privacy statement;
- a clear description of submission/review limits;
- authentic, permissioned feedback examples;
- accurate “last updated” information.

Do not simulate enterprise logos, learner volume, ratings, recent activity, or testimonials.

### Motion

- Use 140–200ms hover and press transitions.
- Prefer subtle progress movement or a single entrance reveal over autoplay marketing carousels.
- Do not animate essential reading content away from the learner.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Avoid motion on long-answer and submission screens where attention should remain on the task.

### Recommended public-page sequence

1. Specific bilingual outcome and primary CTA.
2. Honest proof/privacy strip.
3. Five-step lesson-review learning loop.
4. Representative lesson and activity preview.
5. Four-plan comparison.
6. Teacher feedback and Premium+ explanation.
7. Authentic trust material.
8. FAQ covering access, billing contact, privacy, and feedback limits.
9. Final CTA.

## Limitations

- This benchmark covers publicly accessible, signed-out pages, not authenticated learner dashboards or teacher tools.
- Promotions, prices, counters, and plan details may change after 2026-08-14.
- NativeCamp’s page loaded and exposed its accessible DOM and computed desktop/mobile layout, but screenshot capture timed out on the JS-heavy homepage.
- Some screenshot attempts on DMM, RareJob, and Cambly caused the corresponding browser tab to close. Their DOM structures and computed layouts were still inspectable. DMM’s 390px pricing screenshot succeeded and confirmed the measured horizontal cropping.
- Bizmates desktop and mobile homepage states were visually inspected; responsive pricing-card behavior was not independently confirmed to the same degree as RareJob and Cambly.
- Carousel presence was confirmed from visible controls or repeated slide groups. Autoplay is not claimed where it was not directly verified.
- The benchmark does not recommend copying proprietary fonts, photography, illustrations, brand marks, page copy, or source code.
